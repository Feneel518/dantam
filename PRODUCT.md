# Product and growth model

Dantam serves independent Indian clinics with one clinic owner and a front desk. The free website brings dentists into the product. Paid automation earns its fee by reducing follow-up work and helping eligible patients book and return.

The current release closes a measurable loop: enquiry → confirmed booking → attended visit → honest review request → clinician-selected recall. Reception staff must keep visit attendance and recall dates current. Retention is only as automatic as that source data. Connecting an existing clinic management system is the next priority after a pilot identifies the system used by most customers.

## Commercial model

Free includes website hosting at the clinic URL and the records needed for a useful pilot. Growth costs ₹2,999 monthly per clinic, with 300 patient messages; Practice costs ₹5,999, with 1,000. Clinics own their Google and WhatsApp accounts. External provider fees are additional, transparent charges. Do not offer unlimited messaging or bundle paid advertising without a defined budget.

Track contribution margin per clinic: subscription receipts minus payment fees, hosting allocation, messaging subsidy (if any), email cost and support time. The chosen initial fees are hypotheses until actual willingness to pay and servicing cost are measured. A five-minute setup that later requires weekly operator work is not an automatic SaaS; include that work in the margin calculation.

## Pilot criteria

Recruit 5–10 clinics in one city or dentist community. Record a comparable 30-day baseline and run the pilot for 60–90 days. Set each clinic's goals from its capacity and baseline rather than promising a universal patient count.

Measure activation (website published, Google connected, messages ready), time to first confirmed enquiry booking, enquiry-to-attended conversion, no-shows, unique returning patients, attributed collected revenue and clinic renewal. Track manual support minutes and automation failures alongside outcomes. Avoid attributing every visit or seasonal change to the software.

Interview the receptionist as well as the dentist. Find out whether appointment updates are easy enough to keep current. If attendance is often missing, stop trusting the retention numbers and integrate the clinic's existing workflow.

## Useful next additions

1. Self-serve Meta Embedded Signup and managed template provisioning after Tech Provider approval. The release currently uses verified clinic credentials entered through Connections; this is a launch onboarding step, not fully self-serve WhatsApp onboarding.
2. Integration with the most common practice software in the pilot to sync attendance, recalls and cancellations automatically.
3. Cancellation waitlist with explicit opt-in, compatible appointment durations and one confirmed slot allocation, to fill unused chair time without double booking.
4. Enquiry response tracking and approved follow-ups for unanswered requests; avoid repeatedly messaging someone who declines.
5. Clinic-owner-approved educational pages based on real services and qualifications. Avoid fabricated testimonials, thin mass-produced location pages or clinical promises.
6. Custom domains, multiple staff roles, multiple chairs and multi-location reporting when paying clinics request them.

Paid campaigns can be an optional later service with explicit budgets and source tracking. A website or automatic review reply alone does not create predictable new-patient demand. Do not advertise guaranteed Google ranking or patient growth; Google's local ranking also depends on distance, relevance and prominence.
